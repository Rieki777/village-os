## What the talk is for

It is a blueprint for a group that has decided to coordinate and has not decided how. The speaker
had been walking several organisations through the same setup and kept hearing the same three
questions, so the talk answers them in order: how do we organise, how do we reward each other, and
how do we decide. The framing throughout is that the tools are primitives and never a prescription.
A group turns the knobs and gets its own organisation out.

## How we organise: roles, circles, badges

Roles and circles belong to the organisation and never to a person. Each one is defined by its
purpose, where it sits, what can be expected of it, what it alone controls, and who is currently
filling it, which may be nobody. Circles nest, and each one links to the circle above it, so the
structure scales by adding circles instead of by adding meeting length. Badges attach to people
and carry bonuses: to salary, or to voice.

The habit worth stealing is the expiry. Every assignment and every badge has an end date, and the
reasoning is behavioural: in a decentralised group it is much easier to not say yes than to say no,
because saying no costs somebody an argument. So nothing renews itself silently. Hypha set three
months as the ceiling and every group sets its own. It also removes the need for anybody to police
the roster: somebody who stops showing up stops being paid when their assignment runs out, and no
proposal has to be written about them.

## How we reward each other: bands, badges, contribution accounting

Salary is attached to the ROLE and badges are attached to the PERSON. The talk is candid about how
Hypha got there. Letting each member name their own number produced a negotiation about worth
instead of a conversation about value, and pricing every role against outside market rates was slow
and did not land anywhere better. Bands replaced both: a role sits in a band by how complex and how
accountable it is, and the band sets the pay. The point is not accuracy but objectivity, because a
transparent organisation cannot hide a salary and therefore needs a reason for it that survives
being read. The advice for a new group is to start with one band and add bands only when the work
actually differs.

Ownership works the same way. Instead of a founder holding an arbitrary hundred percent and giving
slices away, the organisation starts at zero and mints tokens as contributions land: intellectual
property brought in, money invested, work done. Every holding is then a record of a contribution
that somebody voted to accept.

## How we decide: three dials

This is the part the Game's engine descends from directly. Governance is three parameters, and the
named styles are corners of the space they describe.

- **Voice variance.** How unevenly the voice token is held. At one end everybody holds one, which
  is one person one vote. At the other, one member holds nearly all of it.
- **Quorum.** How much has to show up for a decision to count.
- **Unity.** How much of what showed up has to agree.

Set them low, high and fifty and you have classic democracy. Set quorum low and unity at a hundred
and you have consent, where a decision passes unless somebody blocks it. Set both at a hundred and
you have consensus. Hypha's own setting is twenty quorum and eighty unity, which the talk calls
distributed voice: fifty is treated as too low, on the argument that if a fifth of the voice thinks
something is a bad idea there is probably a flaw worth fixing. High variance with low quorum and a
simple majority is an oligarchy. One holder with everything is a dictatorship. No quorum and no
unity is anarchy, where nothing can be blocked and nothing can be relied on. The talk names all of
them without judgement and says which context each one suits.

Two arguments in that section are worth carrying into any village that copies the dials. Quorum
exists because people find it easier to stay quiet than to object, so a decision that nobody
stopped is not the same as a decision anybody supported. And unity is a dial with a cost at both
ends: too high and one member can freeze the group, too low and the group stops hearing its own
dissent. A tightly bounded membership can afford consent or consensus. An open one probably cannot,
because anybody can walk in and block.

Two smaller dials sit beside the three: how long a vote runs, and how long an assignment or a badge
lasts before it has to be renewed.

## The meetings

Three containers, deliberately kept apart. One for governance, where the rules and the dials
themselves are changed. One for the human side, which is where a group builds enough trust to
disagree well. One for operations, which is where work gets done. The reasoning is practical: if
there is no container for a governance tension, it surfaces in the operational meeting and
frustrates everybody who came to get something finished.

## The two tokens

The talk's sharpest point for this project. Most organisations in the space use one token for
governance and for value, which means the token that decides is the token money can buy, and the
feedback loop concentrates power in whoever has the most money. Hypha separates them: a tradable
value token, and a voice token that is earned, locked to the account that earned it, and never
transferable. A third token handles payments. The consequence the talk draws out is that voice can
be given to somebody the organisation wants deciding without giving them any financial stake at
all, and it names bioregional governance and indigenous elders as the case where that matters.

## Where this project agrees and where it does not

Stated plainly, because a source is more useful when the distance from it is visible.

- The three dials, the named corners, the expiry habit, the two-token separation, and the argument
  for why quorum exists are all carried into this platform's engine as they are.
- The Voice token here is not transferable, which follows the talk.
- Voice here CAN be bought. The founder ruled on 2026-09-03 that money in mints Voice by default,
  and that transparency is the protection. That is a deliberate departure from the talk's own
  reasoning, and `docs/GOVERNANCE.md` says so in the section on voting weight.
- This platform's steward seat, its veto window and its landing instants have no equivalent in the
  talk. They come from this project's own rulings, which are recorded in
  `docs/GOVERNANCE_EVOLUTION_PROMPT.md`.
