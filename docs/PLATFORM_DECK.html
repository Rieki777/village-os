Where it hurts

Nine tools, and none of them know each other.

Most projects coordinate across a chat, a spreadsheet, a shared drive, a form builder, a payment link, a calendar, a group thread, and somebody's memory. Each one works. None of them knows that the person who ran the water system for six months is the same person asking to move onto the land.

So contribution goes unrecorded, roles live in one founder's head, money arrives with no trace of what it bought, and the honest question of what a person has given and what the village has given back has no answer anyone can look up.

Every one of those tools is fine on its own. What is missing is one place that holds the whole picture.

Value

A ledger that cannot quietly be wrong.

Recognition, credits and every village token move through one ledger with one rule: for each token, all balances sum to zero.

Value is never created by a line of code. It comes out of a named source and lands in a named account, and both are visible.

Conservation
Asserted at every boot. If the books do not balance, the server refuses to start rather than serve a number it cannot stand behind.
Balances
Recomputed from the entries, never incremented. There is no counter to drift.
Every movement
Carries an idempotency key, so a retried request is the same act rather than a second one.
A trade
Is exactly two legs in one transaction, both or neither. No half-applied swap exists.
Recognition
Is earned, full stop. It can never be bought, and the code has no path to sell it.

The point is not that the software is careful. The point is that when a member asks where their contribution went, someone can answer with a record instead of a recollection.

Authority

One answer to the question of who may do this.

Permission is decided in a single place, in a fixed order, so authority can be reasoned about rather than discovered.

1Admin
Outranks everything, including a warning.
2Warning badge
Outranks role and stage. A member under review loses the specific thing under review and keeps everything else.
3Appointed role
The seat someone was given, with a term.
4Earned badge
What they proved they can do.
5Stage
How far along they are.

Nothing else in the system is allowed to invent its own permission check.

Stages
The path from guest to co-creator, with capabilities that unlock along the way.
Roles
Appointed seats with terms, such as the water keeper or the kitchen steward, visible on the village map with the open ones marked.
Badges
Earned from settled contribution, or granted as honours. Earned ones cannot be handed out.
Leaving
A member can go. What they are owed is enumerated and settled, their record anonymised, the ledger left intact.

Money

Four doors money comes in by.

All of them ride one payment path: one webhook, verified against the raw body, deduped per event, with mechanical reversals.

A refund or a chargeback claws back exactly what that charge delivered, and only if it was actually delivered.

Payments
Products you define: application fees, donations, deposits and down payments, waitlist seats, recurring memberships, token packs.
Stays
Accommodation priced in credits, bought or earned through work-exchange quests. One credit hosts one night.
Exchange
Members buy the village's own tokens from a stocked treasury. Token-to-token trading exists and stays off until a village turns it on deliberately.
Library
Donate a tool and earn credits, borrow against a deposit held in escrow. The credits are backed by what is on the shelf.
The line that does not move

Money flows in. Tokens never flow out.

There is no path in this software to sell a village token back for cash, and building one is not a setting. Equity and voice live where they belong, governed elsewhere, shown here read-only, never traded.

The land

The land keeps its own ledger.

Trees planted, hectares under care, water retained. Absolute counts, recorded by the people who walked the site, never a leaderboard.

Alongside them, a snapshot of the village's vital signs frozen at the close of every lunar cycle, so a season can be compared to the one before it rather than to a feeling.

Together

Villages that can find each other.

Every deployment can publish what it needs and what it has spare, and read what peer villages publish.

One project's idle excavator and another's unfilled week of masonry are the same problem twice, unsolved only because nobody could see both. You choose exactly what leaves your instance. Federation is opt-in, and a village that shares nothing loses nothing.

Honestly

What this is not.

It is not a currency scheme.

Tokens here buy nights, tools and access inside one village. They are not investments, they do not leave, and the software will not help you pretend otherwise.

It is not finished.

Some things are deliberately unbuilt pending legal advice. Expiring member credits, for one, where the setting exists and the platform refuses to accept a value rather than promise something it will not do.

It is not hosted for you by default.

You run it, unless you ask us to. That is the point: your database, your keys, your members' data, and a copy of the source you can read, change, or walk away with.

Common questions

Before you apply.

What happens to our data?
It lives in your database, on your hosting, under your keys. We do not hold a copy after handoff, and your API keys never touch our database.
Do we have to switch everything on?
No. Four modules are always on. The other twenty-two ship off, and turning one on is a deliberate act recorded with who did it and when. Most projects start with a handful.
Can our members cash out their tokens?
No. Money flows in and tokens never flow out. There is no code path to sell a village token back for cash, and building one is not a setting.
What if we want to leave?
You already have everything: the source, the database, the domain and the keys. Nothing needs our permission, and after handoff we change nothing unless you ask.
